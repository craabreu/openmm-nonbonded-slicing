Performance¶

The tables below compare SlicedNonbondedForce with the openmm.NonbondedForce it replaces in two systems of about 50,000 atoms, using PME with a 1 nm cutoff on the CUDA platform in mixed precision (NVIDIA RTX 4090, OpenMM 8.6.1 with the reduction of parameter derivatives on the device from openmm/openmm#5520). With n subsets, the interactions between subset 0 and each other subset are scaled by two parameters, one for Coulomb and one for Lennard-Jones, as one would do to decouple a ligand from its environment. A step is a LangevinMiddleIntegrator step of 2 fs, which computes only forces. An evaluation is a call to getState with getForces=True and getEnergy=True. Columns marked with derivatives request the derivatives with respect to all 2(n-1) parameters and, for evaluations, pass getParameterDerivatives=True. Times are in milliseconds and can be reproduced with devtools/benchmarks/benchmark.py.

The first system is a box of 16,814 TIP3P water molecules (50,442 atoms, 8 nm) split into n contiguous blocks of whole molecules.

The cost of a step grows sublinearly with the number of subsets: going from 1 to 8 subsets makes a step about 3.4 times as expensive. Since derivatives are only computed along with the energy, requesting them barely affects steps (9% with 14 derivatives). The cost of an evaluation with derivatives grows with their number, not with the number of slices: a parameter that scales several slices adds a single derivative. Derivatives are best requested only for the parameters whose energies are needed, and only in the steps where they are reported. With OpenMM releases that sum the derivatives on the host, each evaluation with derivatives costs about 0.5 ms more, regardless of the system size.
