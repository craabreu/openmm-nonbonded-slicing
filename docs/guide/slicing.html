Subsets, slices and scaling parameters¶

What it does¶

A SlicedNonbondedForce computes the same Coulomb and Lennard-Jones interactions as a openmm.NonbondedForce, with the same nonbonded methods, cutoffs, exceptions and parameter offsets. In addition, each particle belongs to one of \(n\) subsets, and the energy is sliced into \(n(n+1)/2\) parts, one for each pair of subsets \(I \le J\): slice \(I, J\) comprises all pairs formed by a particle in subset \(I\) and a particle in subset \(J\). The Coulomb and Lennard-Jones contributions of every slice can be multiplied by a scaling parameter, the value of a openmm.Context global parameter:

\[ E = \sum_{I \le J} \left( \lambda^{vdW}_{I,J} E^{vdW}_{I,J} + \lambda^{elec}_{I,J} E^{elec}_{I,J} \right). \]

Scaling parameters are constant and equal to 1 unless you say otherwise, so a force with no scaling parameters yields the energy of the NonbondedForce it replaces. Lennard-Jones interactions use the Lorentz-Berthelot combining rule: the arithmetic mean of the sigmas and the geometric mean of the epsilons of the two particles.

Setting it up¶

A SlicedNonbondedForce is created either from scratch, by specifying the number of subsets and calling addParticle for every particle, or from an existing NonbondedForce, whose particles, exceptions, global parameters, offsets and settings it inherits. Every particle starts in subset 0; setParticleSubset moves it. A scaling parameter is a global parameter, declared with addGlobalParameter, whose name is passed to addScalingParameter together with the slice it applies to and the contributions it multiplies:

import openmm as mm
import nonbondedslicing as nbs

force = nbs.SlicedNonbondedForce(nonbonded, 2)   # built from a NonbondedForce, 2 subsets
for atom in ligand_atoms:
    force.setParticleSubset(atom, 1)             # the rest stays in subset 0
force.addGlobalParameter("lambda", 1.0)
force.addScalingParameter("lambda", 0, 1, True, True)   # scale Coulomb and LJ of slice (0, 1)
system.removeForce(index)                        # index of the NonbondedForce being replaced
system.addForce(force)

context = mm.Context(system, integrator)
context.setParameter("lambda", 0.5)              # halve the ligand-environment interactions

The order of the two subsets in addScalingParameter is irrelevant. The same parameter can be applied to any number of slices, and a slice can take one parameter for its Coulomb part and another for its Lennard-Jones part. A sixth argument, includeDecapping, makes a parameter multiply the decapping term of its slice; see Capped Lennard-Jones potential.

Restrictions¶

  • A scaling parameter must have been added with addGlobalParameter, and it cannot double as the parameter of a particle or exception parameter offset.

  • Every call to addScalingParameter must give the parameter at least one role in the slice: Coulomb (includeCoulomb), Lennard-Jones (includeLJ) or decapping (includeDecapping), and each role of a slice belongs to at most one parameter. A second parameter for a role that is already taken is an error.

  • The set of scaling parameters and their roles are read when a Context is created. Changes made afterwards with addScalingParameter or setScalingParameter take effect only after the Context’s reinitialize() method is called. The values of the parameters, in contrast, are read at every evaluation and can be changed at any time with the Context’s setParameter method.

  • Particle parameters, exception parameters and particle subsets can be changed in an existing Context with updateParametersInContext, with the same limitations as in NonbondedForce: other settings, such as the nonbonded method and the cutoff, are not updated (reinitialize the Context instead), the pair of particles of an exception cannot change, and particles or exceptions cannot be added.
