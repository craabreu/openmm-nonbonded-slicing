Capped Lennard-Jones potential¶

What it does¶

Driving the Lennard-Jones scaling parameter of a slice to zero with the usual potential leaves a singularity at \(r = 0\) that makes free-energy calculations hard to converge. A scaling parameter added with includeDecapping=True multiplies the decapping term of its slice, which splits each Lennard-Jones pair of the slice into a capped term and a decapping term [1], in the spirit of the capping of [2] and the linear basis functions of [3]:

\[ U(r) = \lambda_{LJ}\, u_{cap}(r) + \lambda_D \left[ u_{LJ}(r) - u_{cap}(r) \right], \]

where \(u_{LJ}\) is the usual potential, \(\lambda_{LJ}\) is the scaling parameter that multiplies the Lennard-Jones contributions of the slice (1 if there is none), \(\lambda_D\) is the decapping parameter, and

\[\begin{split} u_{cap}(r) = \begin{cases} 4 \epsilon\, c(r/\sigma), & r < \sigma \\ u_{LJ}(r), & r \ge \sigma \end{cases} \end{split}\]

is a polynomial core. It is defined by two orders, \(m_0\) and \(m_1\): \(c(x)\) is the polynomial of degree \(m_0 + m_1\) whose first \(m_0\) derivatives vanish at \(x = 0\) and whose value and first \(m_1\) derivatives match those of \(x^{-12} - x^{-6}\) at \(x = 1\). The first condition leaves the terms \(a_0\) and \(a_k x^k\) for \(k = m_0 + 1, \dots, m_0 + m_1\), and the second fixes their \(m_1 + 1\) coefficients. The core thus joins the usual potential smoothly, up to the \(m_1\)-th derivative, at \(r = \sigma\) and takes the finite value \(4 \epsilon\, a_0\) at \(r = 0\). The default orders, \(m_0 = m_1 = 3\), give

\[ c(x) = 29.8 - 360 x^4 + 547.2 x^5 - 217 x^6, \]

with a core of \(119.2\,\epsilon\). The decapping term vanishes for \(r \ge \sigma\), and \(\lambda_D = \lambda_{LJ}\) gives the usual potential.

The energy is linear in both parameters. The derivative with respect to \(\lambda_{LJ}\) is the capped van der Waals energy of the slice, the one with respect to \(\lambda_D\) is its decapping energy, and their sum is the usual van der Waals energy (Energy parameter derivatives). One evaluation therefore gives the energy at any pair of values, which is what reweighting estimators such as MBAR need.

Setting it up¶

force.addGlobalParameter("lambdaQ", 1.0)
force.addGlobalParameter("lambdaLJ", 1.0)
force.addGlobalParameter("lambdaD", 1.0)
force.addScalingParameter("lambdaQ", 0, 1, True, False)
force.addScalingParameter("lambdaLJ", 0, 1, False, True)
force.addScalingParameter("lambdaD", 0, 1, False, False, True)

A coupling path switches on the capped term first (\(\lambda_{LJ}\) from 0 to 1 with \(\lambda_D = 0\)), then the decapping term, and finally the Coulomb term. The stages can overlap: [1] couples all three in one sweep of a master parameter \(\lambda\) with smoothstep functions \(h_k(\lambda)\) that start and finish at \((0, 0.7)\), \((0.4, 0.8)\) and \((0.6, 1.0)\) for the capped, decapping and Coulomb terms. OpenMM cannot make one global parameter a function of another, so set lambdaLJ, lambdaD and lambdaQ from \(\lambda\) at each state; for \(\lambda\)-dynamics, apply the chain rule to the three derivatives.

The capped term is the usual potential beyond \(\sigma\), so the cutoff, the switching function, LJPME and the dispersion correction apply to it exactly and are multiplied by \(\lambda_{LJ}\). The decapping term is not switched, so with a switching function the \(\sigma\) of every Lennard-Jones pair of a decapped slice (pairs with \(\epsilon = 0\) excluded) cannot exceed the switching distance; this is checked when a Context is created and by updateParametersInContext, but not for parameter offsets. Decapping is available on all platforms and with every nonbonded method, for direct-space pairs and 1-4 exceptions. Like the other properties of a scaling parameter, the decapping role and the capping orders are fixed when a openmm.Context is created, while \(\lambda_{LJ}\) and \(\lambda_D\) are its global parameters.

Restrictions¶

  • Only one scaling parameter per slice can multiply its decapping term.

  • The capped core is finite, so charges of opposite sign can collapse onto it. Turn the Coulomb contribution of a slice on only while \(\lambda_D > 0\).

  • With the default orders, the height of the core, \(119.2\,\epsilon\), is only a few \(k_B T\) for atoms with small \(\epsilon\), which then overlap often at \(\lambda_D = 0\). The decapping energy of such overlaps is large, so raise the core with setCappingOrders or place enough states near \(\lambda_D = 0\) to keep the estimates converged. Higher orders give larger coefficients that partly cancel near \(r = \sigma\), which costs some single-precision accuracy there.

  • With an inner force group (Inner force group for multiple time stepping), the inner group takes all of the capped and decapping terms below \(\sigma\). The \(\sigma\) of every Lennard-Jones pair of a decapped slice (pairs with \(\epsilon = 0\) excluded) cannot exceed the inner switching distance. This is checked when a Context is created and by updateParametersInContext, but not for parameter offsets.
