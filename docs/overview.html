Overview¶

This OpenMM plugin implements a sliced variant of OpenMM’s NonbondedForce class. By partitioning all particles among $n$ disjoint subsets, the total potential energy becomes a linear combination of contributions from pairs of subsets like

\[E = \sum_{I=0}^{n-1} \sum_{J=I}^{n-1} (\lambda^{vdW}_{I,J}E^{vdW}_{I,J}+\lambda^{elec}_{I,J}E^{elec}_{I,J}),\]

where each slice is defined by subsets I and J, superscripts vdW and elec denote van der Waals and electrostatic contributions, \(E_{I,J}\) is the potential energy of all particle pairs formed by one particle in subset I and one in subset J, and \(\lambda_{I,J}\) is a scaling parameter.

The nonbonded energy can be partitioned along a second, independent axis. Slicing by subset, above, decides which particle pairs a scaling parameter applies to. Slicing by distance, through an optional inner force group (see below), splits every pair interaction into a short-range part, switched off smoothly between an inner switching distance and an inner cutoff, and a remainder, so that a multiple time-step integrator can advance the two with different time steps. The two partitions compose exactly,

\[E = \sum_{I \le J} \sum_{t \in \{vdW, elec\}} \lambda^{t}_{I,J} \left( E^{t,\mathrm{inner}}_{I,J} + E^{t,\mathrm{outer}}_{I,J} \right),\]

because the inner part of a pair interaction is scaled by the same parameters as the rest of it, and each force group reports an energy consistent with its force.

By default, all scaling parameters are constant and equal to 1. However, the user can turn selected scaling parameters into variables and store their values in openmm.Context global parameters. Derivatives with respect to these variables can be requested and used, for instance, to report individual energy slice contributions or sums thereof via openmm.Context’s getState method with options getEnergy=True and getParameterDerivatives=True. Derivatives are only computed along with the energy: without getEnergy=True, they are NaN.

A global parameter can scale the electrostatic contributions, the van der Waals contributions, or both, of any number of slices. Unless a slice uses the soft-core potential (see below), the energy is linear in the scaling parameters, and the derivative with respect to a parameter \(\lambda\) is the sum of the unscaled contributions it multiplies:

\[\frac{\partial E}{\partial \lambda} = \sum_{(I,J) \in S^{elec}_\lambda} E^{elec}_{I,J} + \sum_{(I,J) \in S^{vdW}_\lambda} E^{vdW}_{I,J},\]

where \(S^{elec}_\lambda\) and \(S^{vdW}_\lambda\) are the sets of slices whose electrostatic and van der Waals contributions are scaled by \(\lambda\). Without soft-core slices, this derivative does not depend on the values of the scaling parameters. For instance, a parameter that scales both contributions of a single slice I, J yields its full energy \(E^{vdW}_{I,J}+E^{elec}_{I,J}\), while one that scales the electrostatic contributions of several slices yields their sum.

Soft-core Lennard-Jones Potential¶

Driving \(\lambda^{vdW}_{I,J}\) to zero with the usual Lennard-Jones potential leaves a singularity at \(r = 0\) that makes free-energy calculations hard to converge. A scaling parameter added with a positive softcoreAlpha switches its slice to the soft-core form of [1], in which each pair interacts through

\[U(r) = 4 \lambda_E \epsilon \left( s^2 - s \right), \qquad s = \frac{1}{\alpha (1 - \lambda_s) + (r/\sigma)^6},\]

where \(\lambda_E\) is the scaling parameter that multiplies the Lennard-Jones contributions of the slice (1 if there is none), \(\lambda_s\) is the softening parameter and \(\alpha\) is softcoreAlpha, typically 0.5. The two parameters can be the same,

force.addScalingParameter("lambda", 0, 1, False, True, 0.5)

or different,

force.addScalingParameter("lambdaE", 0, 1, False, True)
force.addScalingParameter("lambdaS", 0, 1, False, False, 0.5)

and a slice can be softened without scaling its energy at all (only the second line above). The potential remains finite as \(r \to 0\) whenever \(\lambda_s < 1\) and \(\alpha > 0\), and it reduces to the usual one when \(\lambda_s = 1\) or \(\alpha = 0\). Keep \(\lambda_s\) between 0 and 1: for \(\lambda_s > 1\), the term \(\alpha (1 - \lambda_s)\) is negative and the potential becomes singular at \(r = \left[ \alpha (\lambda_s - 1) \right]^{1/6} \sigma\). Since only the Lennard-Jones term is softened, scale the Coulomb contribution of the slice with its own parameter (includeCoulomb=True, includeLJ=False) and turn it off before softening the Lennard-Jones term. Like the other properties of a scaling parameter, \(\alpha\) is fixed when a openmm.Context is created, while \(\lambda_E\) and \(\lambda_s\) are its global parameters. Soft-core slices cannot be combined with an inner force group (see below).

The derivatives with respect to the two parameters are

\[\frac{\partial U}{\partial \lambda_E} = 4 \epsilon (s^2 - s), \qquad \frac{\partial U}{\partial \lambda_s} = 4 \lambda_E \epsilon \alpha s^2 (2 s - 1),\]

and a parameter that plays both roles gets their sum. The soft-core form applies to the direct-space Lennard-Jones term and to 1-4 exceptions, including the additive potential shift at the cutoff with LJPME; the reciprocal-space part of LJPME and the long-range dispersion correction are not softened, an approximation accurate to \(O\left(\alpha (1-\lambda_s) (\sigma/r_c)^6\right)\). As with the usual potential, do not combine LJPME with a switching function: the Reference platform ignores the switching function, while the CUDA and OpenCL platforms apply it to the direct-space Lennard-Jones term, as OpenMM’s NonbondedForce does on each platform.

Warning

For a softened slice, \(\partial U / \partial \lambda_E\) is the soft-core van der Waals energy of the slice, which differs from the usual one whenever \(\lambda_s < 1\), and \(\partial U / \partial \lambda_s\) is not an energy. For a parameter that plays both roles, the reported derivative is the sum of the two, which is not an energy even at \(\lambda = 1\), where the soft-core and usual potentials coincide. To obtain the usual van der Waals energy of the slice, evaluate it in a separate force without the soft-core potential.

With Ewald, PME, or LJPME, the reciprocal-space part can be computed in a separate force group by calling setReciprocalSpaceForceGroup, as in multiple time stepping. As in OpenMM’s NonbondedForce, the terms that subtract the reciprocal-space interactions of excluded pairs and exceptions are computed in the direct-space force group by default. Since they only cancel part of the reciprocal-space sum, they should be integrated along with it whenever the two groups are evaluated at different times [2, 3]. Calling setReciprocalExclusions(True) moves them to the reciprocal-space force group, where they are also kept when direct space is turned off with setIncludeDirectSpace(False).

Force splitting for multiple time-step integration¶

A SlicedNonbondedForce can place a short-range, force-switched copy of its direct-space pair interactions in a separate inner force group, so that a multiple time-step integrator can advance it with a small step while the remainder (the rest of direct space, the reciprocal-space sum and its exclusion corrections) is advanced with a large one. For a pair at distance \(r\) with bare potential \(V(r)\) (Lennard-Jones plus Coulomb, scaled by the slice’s parameters), the inner force is

\[\mathbf{F}^\mathrm{inner}(r) = -S(r)\,V'(r)\,\hat{\mathbf{r}}, \qquad S = 1 - 10 z^3 + 15 z^4 - 6 z^5, \quad z = \frac{r - r_s}{r_c - r_s},\]

with \(S = 1\) for \(r \le r_s\) and \(S = 0\) for \(r \ge r_c\), where \(r_s\) and \(r_c\) are the inner switching and cutoff distances. The inner energy is the potential \(\hat V\) whose gradient is this force, shifted so that \(\hat V(r_c) = 0\) [4]; the direct-space group reports \(V - \hat V\) and the corresponding force. Every group therefore has an energy consistent with its force, and the groups add up to the unsplit result. The closed form of \(\hat V\) used by all platforms is derived in the theory section. The same applies to 1-4 exceptions. Scaling parameters, their derivatives and parameter offsets apply to both parts.

force = nonbondedslicing.SlicedNonbondedForce(nonbonded, 2)
force.setForceGroup(2)               # direct-space remainder; reciprocal space goes there too
force.setInnerForceGroup(1)
force.setInnerSwitchingDistance(0.5*unit.nanometer)
force.setInnerCutoffDistance(0.8*unit.nanometer)
# bonded forces in group 0; steps of 90, 3 and 0.5 fs
integrator = openmm.MTSIntegrator(90*unit.femtoseconds, [(2, 1), (1, 30), (0, 180)])

The inner force group must differ from the force group of the force and, with Ewald, PME or LJPME, from its reciprocal-space force group. It requires \(0 < r_s < r_c \le\) cutoff distance and, when the Lennard-Jones switching function is used, \(r_c \le\) switching distance: the inner force is the bare Lennard-Jones force times \(S(r)\), which beyond the switching distance would exceed the switched force and leave the remainder with a correction of opposite sign. Since the split assumes the bare Lennard-Jones potential, the inner force group cannot be used with soft-core scaling parameters (a positive softcoreAlpha). The inner force group, switching distance and cutoff distance are fixed when a openmm.Context is created; changing them afterwards takes effect only after the Context’s reinitialize() method is called.

On the CUDA and OpenCL platforms, all nonbonded interactions in one force group must use the same cutoff, so a Context fails to be created if the inner force group also contains another cutoff-based nonbonded force, such as a openmm.CustomNonbondedForce, with a different cutoff. Put the inner force group in a group of its own, or in one whose other nonbonded forces use the inner cutoff.

Installation¶

The plugin is distributed on conda-forge:

mamba install -c conda-forge openmm-nonbonded-slicing

Building from Source¶

Requirements:

  • OpenMM 8.4 or later

  • CMake 3.17 or later and a C++ compiler

  • SWIG, the same version that built your OpenMM (OpenMM’s conda-forge builds 8.4 and 8.5/8.6 used SWIG 4.4 and 4.5, respectively)

  • Optional: OpenCL headers for the OpenCL platform, and a CUDA toolkit with NVRTC and cuFFT for the CUDA platform

In a conda environment where OpenMM is installed:

mkdir build && cd build
cmake ..
make install
make PythonInstall

Test Cases¶

From the build directory, run the C++ tests with make test (or ctest) and the Python tests with make PythonTest.
