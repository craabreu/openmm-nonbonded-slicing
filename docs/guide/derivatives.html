Energy parameter derivatives¶

What it does¶

addEnergyParameterDerivative requests the derivative of the force’s energy with respect to a scaling parameter. The derivatives are returned by the getEnergyParameterDerivatives method of a openmm.State obtained with both getEnergy=True and getParameterDerivatives=True:

force.addGlobalParameter("lambda", 1.0)
force.addScalingParameter("lambda", 0, 1, True, True)
force.addEnergyParameterDerivative("lambda")
...
state = context.getState(getEnergy=True, getParameterDerivatives=True)
energy_01 = state.getEnergyParameterDerivatives()["lambda"]

Only scaling parameters qualify: requesting the derivative with respect to another global parameter raises an exception.

What the derivatives mean¶

For a parameter that multiplies the contributions of slices, the energy is linear in the parameter, and its derivative is the sum of the unscaled contributions it multiplies,

\[ \frac{\partial E}{\partial \lambda} = \sum_{(I,J) \in S^{elec}_\lambda} E^{elec}_{I,J} + \sum_{(I,J) \in S^{vdW}_\lambda} E^{vdW}_{I,J}, \]

where \(S^{elec}_\lambda\) and \(S^{vdW}_\lambda\) are the sets of slices whose electrostatic and van der Waals contributions are scaled by \(\lambda\). This derivative does not depend on the values of the scaling parameters. A parameter that scales both contributions of a single slice \(I, J\) yields its full energy \(E^{vdW}_{I,J} + E^{elec}_{I,J}\), while one that scales the electrostatic contributions of several slices yields their sum. This is how the energy of a slice, or of a group of slices, can be reported.

The energy remains linear with decapped slices (Capped Lennard-Jones potential). The derivative with respect to the Lennard-Jones scaling parameter of a decapped slice is its capped van der Waals energy, and the derivative with respect to its decapping parameter is its decapping energy; their sum is the usual van der Waals energy of the slice. A parameter that plays both roles in a slice yields the usual van der Waals energy, as it would without decapping.

Restrictions¶

Important

Derivatives are only computed along with the energy. A State requested with getParameterDerivatives=True but without getEnergy=True holds NaN for every requested derivative, on all platforms. If other forces in the System also contribute derivatives with respect to the same parameter, their sum is NaN too. In exchange, steps that compute only forces, such as regular molecular dynamics steps, do not pay for the derivatives. Expressions like deriv(energy, lambda) in a openmm.CustomIntegrator always compute the energy, so they work as usual.

The cost of an evaluation with derivatives grows with the number of requested derivatives, not with the number of slices, since a parameter that scales several slices adds a single derivative; see Performance. Request derivatives only for the parameters whose energies you need.
