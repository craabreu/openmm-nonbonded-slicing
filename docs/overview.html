Overview¶

This OpenMM plugin implements SlicedNonbondedForce, a variant of OpenMM’s openmm.NonbondedForce. By partitioning all particles among \(n\) disjoint subsets, the total nonbonded potential energy becomes a linear combination of contributions from pairs of subsets,

\[ E = \sum_{I=0}^{n-1} \sum_{J=I}^{n-1} \left( \lambda^{vdW}_{I,J} E^{vdW}_{I,J} + \lambda^{elec}_{I,J} E^{elec}_{I,J} \right), \]

where each slice is defined by subsets \(I\) and \(J\), superscripts vdW and elec denote van der Waals and electrostatic contributions, \(E_{I,J}\) is the potential energy of all particle pairs formed by one particle in subset \(I\) and one in subset \(J\), and \(\lambda_{I,J}\) is a scaling parameter. With this decomposition in place, the plugin offers four capabilities.

Scaling slices. By default, every scaling parameter is 1 and the force yields the same energy as the NonbondedForce it replaces. Selected scaling parameters can instead be tied to openmm.Context global parameters, so that the van der Waals and electrostatic contributions of chosen slices are scaled up or down while a simulation runs. See Subsets, slices and scaling parameters.

Capped Lennard-Jones potential. A scaling parameter can multiply the decapping term of its slice, which splits its Lennard-Jones interactions into a capped term, finite as particles overlap, and the rest of the usual potential, so that a slice can be decoupled alchemically without the singularity of the usual potential while the energy stays linear in every parameter. See Capped Lennard-Jones potential.

Energy parameter derivatives. The derivative of the energy with respect to a scaling parameter can be requested along with the energy, which gives the energy of a slice or of a group of slices. See Energy parameter derivatives.

Force-group splitting. With Ewald, PME and LJPME, the reciprocal-space sum can be computed in its own force group, together with the terms that correct it for excluded pairs and exceptions (Reciprocal-space force group and exclusion corrections). With any nonbonded method, a short-range, force-switched copy of the direct-space interactions can be computed in an inner force group, so that a multiple time-step integrator advances it with a smaller step than the rest (Inner force group for multiple time stepping).

The formulation behind the sliced lattice sums and the inner force group is in Theory, measured costs are in Performance, and the class is documented in Python API.
