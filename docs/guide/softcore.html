Soft-core Lennard-Jones potential¶

What it does¶

Driving the Lennard-Jones scaling parameter of a slice to zero with the usual potential leaves a singularity at \(r = 0\) that makes free-energy calculations hard to converge. A scaling parameter added with a positive softcoreAlpha switches its slice to the soft-core form of [1], in which each pair interacts through

\[ U(r) = 4 \lambda_E \epsilon \left( s^2 - s \right), \qquad s = \frac{1}{\alpha (1 - \lambda_s) + (r/\sigma)^6}, \]

where \(\lambda_E\) is the scaling parameter that multiplies the Lennard-Jones contributions of the slice (1 if there is none), \(\lambda_s\) is the softening parameter and \(\alpha\) is softcoreAlpha, typically 0.5. The potential remains finite as \(r \to 0\) whenever \(\lambda_s < 1\) and \(\alpha > 0\), and it reduces to the usual one when \(\lambda_s = 1\) or \(\alpha = 0\).

Setting it up¶

The energy and softening parameters can be the same,

force.addScalingParameter("lambda", 0, 1, False, True, 0.5)

or different,

force.addScalingParameter("lambdaE", 0, 1, False, True)
force.addScalingParameter("lambdaS", 0, 1, False, False, 0.5)

and a slice can be softened without scaling its energy at all (only the second line above). Since only the Lennard-Jones term is softened, scale the Coulomb contribution of the slice with its own parameter (includeCoulomb=True, includeLJ=False) and turn it off before softening the Lennard-Jones term. Like the other properties of a scaling parameter, \(\alpha\) is fixed when a openmm.Context is created, while \(\lambda_E\) and \(\lambda_s\) are its global parameters.

The soft-core potential is available on all platforms and with every nonbonded method. It applies to the direct-space Lennard-Jones term and to 1-4 exceptions, including the additive potential shift at the cutoff with LJPME. The reciprocal-space part of LJPME and the long-range dispersion correction are not softened, an approximation accurate to \(O\left(\alpha (1-\lambda_s) (\sigma/r_c)^6\right)\).

Restrictions¶

  • Keep \(\lambda_s\) between 0 and 1. For \(\lambda_s > 1\), the term \(\alpha (1 - \lambda_s)\) is negative and the potential becomes singular at \(r = \left[ \alpha (\lambda_s - 1) \right]^{1/6} \sigma\).

  • Only one scaling parameter per slice can soften it.

  • As with the usual potential, do not combine LJPME with a switching function: the Reference platform ignores the switching function, while the CUDA and OpenCL platforms apply it to the direct-space Lennard-Jones term, as OpenMM’s NonbondedForce does on each platform.

  • Soft-core slices cannot be combined with an inner force group (Inner force group for multiple time stepping), whose split assumes the bare Lennard-Jones potential.
