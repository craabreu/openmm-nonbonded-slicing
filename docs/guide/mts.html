Inner force group for multiple time stepping¶

What it does¶

A SlicedNonbondedForce can place a short-range, force-switched copy of its direct-space pair interactions in a separate inner force group, so that a multiple time-step integrator can advance it with a small step while the remainder (the rest of direct space, the reciprocal-space sum and its exclusion corrections) is advanced with a large one. For a pair at distance \(r\) with bare potential \(V(r)\) (Lennard-Jones plus Coulomb, scaled by the slice’s parameters), the inner force is

\[ \mathbf{F}^\mathrm{inner}(r) = -S(r)\,V'(r)\,\hat{\mathbf{r}}, \qquad S = 1 - 10 z^3 + 15 z^4 - 6 z^5, \quad z = \frac{r - r_s}{r_c - r_s}, \]

with \(S = 1\) for \(r \le r_s\) and \(S = 0\) for \(r \ge r_c\), where \(r_s\) and \(r_c\) are the inner switching and cutoff distances. The inner energy is the potential \(\hat V\) whose gradient is this force, shifted so that \(\hat V(r_c) = 0\) [4]; the direct-space group reports \(V - \hat V\) and the corresponding force. Every group therefore has an energy consistent with its force, and the groups add up to the unsplit result. The same applies to 1-4 exceptions, and scaling parameters, their derivatives and parameter offsets apply to both parts. The closed form of \(\hat V\) used by all platforms is derived in Theory.

Setting it up¶

force = nbs.SlicedNonbondedForce(nonbonded, 2)
force.setForceGroup(2)               # direct-space remainder; reciprocal space goes there too
force.setInnerForceGroup(1)
force.setInnerSwitchingDistance(0.5*unit.nanometer)
force.setInnerCutoffDistance(0.8*unit.nanometer)
# bonded forces in group 0; steps of 90, 3 and 0.5 fs
integrator = mm.MTSIntegrator(90*unit.femtoseconds, [(2, 1), (1, 30), (0, 180)])

If the reciprocal-space sum is also moved to a group of its own with setReciprocalSpaceForceGroup, call setReciprocalExclusions(True) so that the exclusion corrections go with it (Reciprocal-space force group and exclusion corrections) and the slow group has no fast intramolecular component.

Restrictions¶

  • The inner force group must differ from the force group of the force and, with Ewald, PME or LJPME, from its reciprocal-space force group.

  • The distances must satisfy \(0 < r_s < r_c \le\) cutoff distance and, when the Lennard-Jones switching function is used, \(r_c \le\) switching distance: the inner force is the bare Lennard-Jones force times \(S(r)\), which beyond the switching distance would exceed the switched force and leave the remainder with a correction of opposite sign.

  • Since the split assumes the bare Lennard-Jones potential, the inner force group cannot be used with soft-core scaling parameters (Soft-core Lennard-Jones potential).

  • The inner force group requires the direct space to be included (the default; see setIncludeDirectSpace).

  • The inner force group, switching distance and cutoff distance are fixed when a openmm.Context is created; changing them afterwards takes effect only after the Context’s reinitialize() method is called.

  • On the CUDA and OpenCL platforms, all nonbonded interactions in one force group must use the same cutoff, so a Context fails to be created if the inner force group also contains another cutoff-based nonbonded force, such as a openmm.CustomNonbondedForce, with a different cutoff. Put the inner force group in a group of its own, or in one whose other nonbonded forces use the inner cutoff.

Performance¶

On the CUDA and OpenCL platforms, the cost of an inner-group evaluation depends little on the inner cutoff, because the inner kernels traverse the neighbor list built for the full cutoff and only skip the pairs beyond \(r_c\). The inner group is therefore not much cheaper than the direct space it is carved from, which limits the speedup of multiple time stepping. The --inner-group mode of devtools/benchmarks/benchmark.py measures it.
